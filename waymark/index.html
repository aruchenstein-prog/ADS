What we believe

Good advice should survive being looked at closely.

These are the rules we work by. They cost us some clients. We think they're why the rest come back.

Show the work

Every recommendation comes with the evidence, the options we rejected, and why. If you can't check our reasoning, you shouldn't trust it.

Say what we don't know

We put a number on our confidence and list the open questions. A confident tone is not the same as being right.

Leave you more capable

You get our methods, templates, and sources. The goal is that you need us less next time.

Count everyone affected

Decisions land on people who aren't in the room: staff, customers, neighbours. We name them and weigh what happens to them.

Disagree out loud

If we think you're about to make a mistake, we'll tell you directly and explain why. Then it's your call, and we'll respect it.

Change our minds in public

When new evidence turns up after a project, we send you an update, even if it makes our earlier advice look wrong.

Words that mean numbers

When we say “likely”, we mean 70 to 90 percent.

Vague words hide disagreement. Every Waymark memo uses this fixed scale, so “probably” means the same thing to you, your board, and us.

We also track how often we're right. Of the calls we rate “likely”, about three in four should hold up. If far more do, we're being too cautious. If far fewer do, we're overconfident, and we'll tell you.

How it works

You see everything as we go.

  1. A free call

    30 minutes. You describe the decision; we tell you honestly whether we can help.

  2. A written scope

    One page: the exact question, what you'll get, the date, and the price. Nothing changes without your sign-off.

  3. Work in the open

    You get a shared folder with our notes and sources from day one, and a short check-in every week.

  4. Handover

    The memo, the evidence, our methods, and our doubts. Plus a follow-up in six months to see how the call held up.
